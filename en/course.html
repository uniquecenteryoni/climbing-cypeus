Lead Climbing in Nature

3–4 days 20 hours of instruction Up to 4 participants

Overview

An intensive, practical course for climbers who want to progress from top-rope climbing to lead climbing in a natural environment.

During the course we will learn and practise the skills needed for safe and responsible lead climbing, from rope management and lead belaying, through clipping and controlled falls, to cleaning an anchor, solving problems and planning an independent climbing day.

The course combines theory, demonstrations and plenty of practical crag practice, with gradual progression according to the participants’ level.

What will we learn?

  • Safety principles in lead climbing
  • Reading and understanding climbing routes
  • Lead climber belaying
  • Correct use of quickdraws
  • Correct clipping and rope management while leading
  • Proper communication between climber and belayer
  • Dynamic belaying and catching falls
  • Controlled falls
  • Cleaning an anchor
  • Solving common problems
  • Planning and managing an independent climbing day
  • Practical practice on routes of different grades

Day 1, Foundations, Safety and Lead Preparation

08:30

Introduction and Level Assessment

Meet the participants, set course goals, review previous experience, check personal equipment and assess basic skills.

09:30

The Safety Chain

Partner checks, knots, communication, rope management, correct use of belay devices and identifying risks.

10:30

Top-Rope Refresher

Practise tying in, belaying, lowering and communicating correctly until the core skills are consistent.

11:30

Lead Equipment and Clipping

Introduction to ropes, runners, carabiners and belay devices. Practise quickdraw orientation, correct clipping and rope management.

12:30

Mock Leading

Practise rope movement, clipping and route decisions while the climber is protected by a top-rope.

Day 2, Lead Belaying and First Lead

08:30

Review and Safety Checks

Review the first day’s skills, partner checks, knots, clipping and rope management.

09:30

Dynamic Belay

Giving and taking rope, belayer positioning, correct movement while belaying and principles of dynamic fall catching.

10:30

First Supervised Lead

Gradual transition from mock leading to leading suitable routes with close instructor supervision.

11:30

Route Management

Choosing clipping points, avoiding back clips and Z-clips, keeping the rope organised and handling different situations while climbing.

12:30

Lead and Belay Practice

Rotate between climber and belayer, practise continuously and receive individual feedback.

Day 3, Falls, Anchor Cleaning and Problem Solving

08:30

Fall Practice

Mental and physical preparation for falling, understanding fall distance and gradually practising controlled falls in suitable conditions.

09:30

Catching a Fall

Practise the belayer’s response to a fall, body position, dynamic belaying and communication with the climber.

10:30

Cleaning an Anchor

Secure attachment, correct work at the anchor, transferring the rope, checking the system and lowering the climber in an orderly way.

11:30

Problem Solving

Practise common situations such as incorrect clipping, a short rope, poor communication, stopping mid-route and changing plans.

12:30

Full Cycle

Lead, belay, take a controlled fall and clean an anchor in sequence, with feedback after each cycle.

Day 4, Final Practice and Independence

08:30

Planning a Climbing Day

Choose routes by participants’ ability and conditions, check weather, required equipment and crag access, and plan for emergencies.

09:30

Full Lead Cycles

Participants complete full climbing cycles: prepare equipment, perform a partner check, lead, belay and clean the anchor under instructor supervision.

10:30

Scenario Practice

Handle situations that may occur during a climbing day and make safe, responsible decisions.

11:30

Skills Assessment

Assess control of equipment, knots, belaying, clipping, rope management, falls, anchor cleaning and responses to changing situations.

12:30

Feedback and Next Steps

Individual feedback, areas for improvement, recommendations for continued practice and clear boundaries for safe progression.

Prerequisites

Experience: Previous experience climbing with top-rope belay

Physical fitness: Good physical condition

Minimum age: 16 years

What to bring: Climbing shoes (if available), comfortable sportswear, water, lunch, sunscreen, hat
