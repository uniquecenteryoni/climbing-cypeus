A practical course built for independence

This course is for top-rope climbers who want to move into outdoor lead climbing in a structured, safe way — with enough practice to understand, not just repeat.

Across three days we build the skills of both a lead climber and a lead belayer: safety checks, rope management, clipping, dynamic belaying, controlled falls, anchor cleaning, problem solving and planning an independent climbing day. Small groups allow repeated practice, personal feedback and close supervision.

Good to know: The final venue is chosen according to weather, shade, rock conditions and group level. The main options are the Dhiarizos Valley or Akamas.

Safety first

Every exercise starts with a briefing, partner checks and progressive practice. The instructor may stop an exercise or change the plan whenever conditions require it.

Learn by doing

Most teaching happens at the crag, with short demonstrations, hands-on practice and feedback after every cycle.

Flights, duration and getting around

Where should I fly?

Paphos Airport (PFO) is recommended because it is convenient for both activity areas. Larnaca (LCA) also works, but requires a longer drive.

How many days?

Three consecutive training days, from 09:00 to 16:00 each day with breaks. This gives approximately 20 hours of practical instruction in total. We recommend arriving the day before and flying home the following day or late in the evening.

  • The exact meeting point and arrival time are sent once the group is confirmed.
  • A rental car is strongly recommended, especially if you stay outside Paphos or Polis.
  • Driving times are estimates and depend on accommodation and the final crag.

Where should I stay?

The best base depends on the final venue. You can book independently, or ask us for guidance by budget and preference.

If the course is in Dhiarizos

The valley is south-east of Paphos, so Paphos or the villages along the approach are practical bases.

  • Paphos — easiest for restaurants, supermarkets and services.
  • Kouklia / Nikokleia — quieter options with a shorter approach.
  • Rural accommodation in the valley — local atmosphere, but check access and car requirements.

If the course is in Akamas

Akamas is in north-west Cyprus. Polis and Latchi are the most convenient bases.

  • Polis Chrysochous — practical base with restaurants, supermarkets and services.
  • Latchi — good if you prefer the sea, harbour and promenade.
  • Neo Chorio — closer to Akamas and a more village-like atmosphere.

Practical recommendation: if you prefer not to change hotels, staying in Paphos and driving each day can work — we will confirm the final plan as early as possible.

What should I bring?

Personal kit

  • Climbing shoes, if you own a pair.
  • Comfortable sportswear and a warm layer for the evening.
  • 2–3 litres of water per person, food and lunch.
  • Hat, sunscreen, sunglasses and a small backpack.
  • Personal medication and relevant medical information.

Technical equipment

  • Harness, helmet and belay device are provided.
  • Rope, quickdraws, slings, carabiners and backup equipment are provided.
  • You may bring personal gear for a pre-course check, but it is not required.
  • Climbing shoes are not included; tell us in advance if you need an alternative.

What is included — and what is not?

Included

  • Three practical training days, approximately 20 hours total.
  • Instructor, safety briefings, demonstrations and personal feedback.
  • All technical equipment needed for the course.
  • Venue selection and programme adjustments for conditions.
  • Final assessment and certificate for participants who meet the requirements.

Not included

  • Flights, transfers and rental car.
  • Accommodation, food, drinks and any site entry fees.
  • Travel insurance and personal insurance covering adventure activities.
  • Climbing shoes and personal equipment not listed as course equipment.

Detailed daily syllabus

Times may shift slightly due to heat, shade, driving and participant level. Water and food breaks are built into every day.

Day 1
Foundations and lead preparation		Opening and level assessment: goals, previous experience, gear check and crag orientation.
Safety chain: knots, partner checks, communication, rope management and belay devices.
Top-rope refresher: tying in, belaying, lowering and commands.
Equipment and clipping: quickdraw orientation, back clips, Z-clips and rope management.
Mock leading: practise clipping and route decisions while protected on top-rope.
Day 2
Lead belaying and first lead		Review and checks: common mistakes and a consistent checking sequence.
Dynamic belay: belayer position, giving and taking rope, movement and catching a fall.
First supervised lead: gradual transition to suitable lead routes.
Route management: clipping points, route reading, organised rope handling and managing fear.
Role rotation: every participant practises as climber and belayer and receives feedback.
Day 3
Falls, cleaning and independence		Controlled falls: mental preparation, fall distance and progressive practice when appropriate.
Catching a fall: belayer response, body position, dynamics and communication.
Cleaning an anchor: secure attachment, rope transfer, system checks and lowering.
Problem solving: short rope, incorrect clip, poor communication, stopping mid-route and changing plans.
Final cycle: lead, belay, fall/controlled exercise and clean an anchor in sequence, followed by feedback.

Certificate of completion

Independent outdoor lead climber

Participants who demonstrate the required safety and lead-climbing skills receive a Climbing Cyprus certificate of completion, confirming that they completed the course and were assessed on the skills covered.

Important: This is not a government licence and does not replace accumulated experience, judgement or a fresh assessment of conditions on every climbing day. Continued gradual practice with an experienced partner is recommended.

Who is the course for?

For climbers who want to take responsibility for their climbing day — from planning to lowering.

  • Previous experience with top-rope climbing and basic belaying.
  • Basic climbing ability and motivation to learn lead; you do not need to be a competition climber.
  • Good general fitness and no medical restriction that would make strenuous activity at height unsafe.
  • Recommended minimum age: 16; under 18s require a parent/guardian consent.
  • Not suitable for anyone looking for a one-off experience without practice, or unwilling to follow safety procedures.

Cancellation and weather policy

This policy balances participant flexibility with reserved places, instructor planning and costs already incurred.

  • 14+ days before: full refund, less any non-refundable payment processing fees.
  • 7–13 days before: 50% refund, or transfer to another date subject to availability.
  • Less than 7 days: no refund; you may request a participant substitution in advance.
  • Instructor cancellation or unsafe weather: a new date will be arranged or a full credit issued. The decision is made on safety grounds only.
  • Flights, accommodation and car hire should be booked flexibly and remain the participant’s responsibility.

Frequently asked questions

I have no gear — can I still join?

Yes. Technical course equipment is provided. Climbing shoes are not required if a suitable alternative is available, but please tell us in advance.

I am afraid of falling. Is this suitable?

Yes, as long as you want to learn. Fall practice is progressive, only done when appropriate, and paced to the individual.

Can I come alone?

Yes. Groups are small and we pair participants for practice during the course.

Can I climb independently immediately after?

The goal is to build a practical foundation for independence, but real independence grows through practice. You will receive clear feedback on what you can safely do and what needs more supervision.

What if I do not meet the certificate requirements?

You will receive feedback and a practice recommendation. Safety comes before the certificate, and in suitable cases a later assessment can be arranged.
